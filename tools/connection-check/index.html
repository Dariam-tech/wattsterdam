Quick answer

Many newer Dutch homes have a 3x25A connection (about 17 kW). That is enough for an all-electric heat pump, an induction stove and an 11 kW EV charger at the same time, as long as the charger has a load balancer. Without one, the same combination can draw over 20 kW and trip your main fuse. Older homes with a single-phase 1x35A connection (about 8 kW) usually can't add a heat pump and an induction stove without an upgrade.

What to do if you're over the limit

  • Add a load balancer to the EV charger. It slows charging when the rest of the house needs power. It's the cheapest fix and often makes an upgrade unnecessary.
  • Choose a hybrid heat pump instead of an all-electric one. It keeps the gas boiler for the coldest days and draws much less power.
  • Apply for a heavier connection with your grid operator (netbeheerder: Liander, Stedin or Enexis, depending on your region). In areas with grid congestion this can take a long time, so check before you order equipment.

How this calculator works

It adds a 3 kW baseline for everyday appliances, 3.5 kW for an induction stove, the heat pump's peak draw and the EV charger's power (zero if a load balancer is fitted), then compares the total with your connection's capacity. Up to 80% of capacity counts as comfortable, 80 to 100% as tight, and above 100% as likely to exceed. It's a rough estimate, not an official assessment; an installer can confirm your specific setup.
