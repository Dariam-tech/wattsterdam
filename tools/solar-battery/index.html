Quick answer

A typical Dutch home with 10 solar panels and 3,300 kWh of yearly use will pay about €425 more per year once saldering ends on 1 January 2027, if its supplier pays the legal minimum for exported power. If the supplier pays 10 cents per kWh, the increase is about €346.

The reason: until the end of 2026, the electricity you feed back is netted against what you take from the grid, up to the amount you take. Each netted kWh is worth your full rate of about €0.26, including energy tax and VAT. From 2027 every kWh you feed back only earns the feed-in payment, which by law is at least half the supply price excluding tax: about €0.06 per kWh at today's average prices.

Does a home battery pay off after 2027?

Usually not. A 10 kWh battery costs about €3,300 to €7,000 installed (Trustoo, October 2026) and saves a typical 10-panel home about €144 a year. Milieu Centraal also concludes that a home battery will most likely not pay for itself through savings on your electricity bill. A battery is worth more if your supplier charges high feed-in costs, if you use a lot of power in the evening, or if you let it trade on hourly prices with a dynamic contract.

Cheaper ways to limit the increase

  • Use your own solar power during the day. Run the dishwasher, washing machine or EV charger when the sun is out. Every kWh you use directly saves your full rate instead of earning the feed-in payment.
  • Compare suppliers on the net feed-in rate. Most large suppliers have published their 2027 rates for new fixed contracts: what you get per kWh minus the feed-in costs ranges from about 0.25 to 5 cents (Zonnesaldo overview, October 2026). Every supplier must list both on its "Modelcontract" page.
  • Consider a dynamic contract if you can shift usage to cheap hours. Feed-in then follows the hourly market price, and several dynamic suppliers charge no feed-in costs.

The rules from 2027

  • Saldering (net metering) ends on 1 January 2027. Your supplier splits your usage and feed-in into the period up to 31 December 2026 and the period after.
  • Until 2030, suppliers must pay at least 50% of the price you pay for electricity without energy tax and VAT for what you feed back.
  • You pay energy tax and VAT on all electricity you take from the grid. Solar power you use directly is not taxed.
  • Suppliers may charge feed-in costs (terugleverkosten). In the model contract they are a fixed amount per kWh, and they can be higher than the feed-in payment.
  • The energy tax on electricity is set at 8.80 cents per kWh excluding VAT for 2027 (about 10.6 cents including VAT) in the Belastingplan 2027, down from 9.16 cents in 2026.

How this calculator works

The saldering effect is the amount that is netted today, the smaller of what you take from the grid and what you feed back, times the difference between your all-in rate and the feed-in payment. Feed-in costs are left out of this comparison because suppliers charge them on everything you feed back, before and after 2027.

If you count panels, we estimate 380 kWh per panel per year and assume you use 30% of it directly, both figures from Milieu Centraal. If you use your bill, we take your actual "levering" and "teruglevering".

The battery follows Milieu Centraal: a home battery raises the share of solar power you use directly from about 30% to just over 50%. We assume a round-trip efficiency of 90%. Each kWh the battery supplies saves your full rate; each kWh it stores no longer earns the feed-in payment minus feed-in costs.

It does not include battery trading on dynamic prices, and real results depend on your roof direction, shading and usage pattern.
