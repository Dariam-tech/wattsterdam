Quick answer

A typical Dutch home with 4 kWp of solar panels and 3,300 kWh of annual use will pay about €565 more per year once saldering ends on 1 January 2027 (at an all-in rate of €0.28/kWh and a feed-in rate of €0.07/kWh). Depending on what your supplier pays for exported electricity, the increase ranges from roughly €480 to €700 a year.

The reason: today every kWh you export cancels out a kWh you buy at the full price, including energy tax and VAT. From 2027 an exported kWh only earns the feed-in rate, which is a fraction of that.

Does a home battery pay off after 2027?

Usually not on saldering savings alone. A 10 kWh battery costs about €3,300 to €7,000 installed (Trustoo, October 2026) and saves a typical 4 kWp home about €290 a year, a simple payback of roughly 11 to 24 years. Many batteries come with a 10-year warranty. A battery makes more sense if you use a lot of electricity in the evening, have a large solar system, or pair it with a dynamic contract and let it trade on hourly prices.

Cheaper ways to limit the increase

  • Use your own solar power during the day. Run the dishwasher, washing machine, heat-pump boiler or EV charger when the sun is out. Every kWh you use directly is worth the full €0.28 instead of the feed-in rate.
  • Check your contract for terugleverkosten. Many fixed contracts charge a fee per exported kWh, which can push your net feed-in rate close to zero. Compare suppliers on the net rate, not the headline one.
  • Consider a dynamic contract if you can shift usage to cheap hours. It's riskier, but feed-in follows the hourly market price and some suppliers charge no feed-in fee.

Key facts about the end of saldering

  • Saldering (net metering) ends on 1 January 2027. This is set in law and applies to every household with solar panels.
  • From 2027 to 2030, suppliers must pay at least 50% of the bare supply rate (excluding tax and VAT) for exported electricity. At a €0.24 all-in rate that is about €0.055/kWh. After 2030 suppliers set their own rate.
  • The energy tax on electricity is proposed at about €0.106/kWh including VAT for 2027 (Belastingplan 2027, still going through parliament), down from about €0.111 in 2026.

How this calculator works

It estimates your solar output at 950 kWh per kWp per year, spread over the months as a typical Dutch system produces it. Each day, solar first covers your daytime use (assumed 35% of your daily consumption); a battery can then store the remaining surplus, up to 90% of its rated size, for that evening and night. The extra cost is every exported kWh times the difference between your all-in rate and the feed-in rate. It does not include battery trading on dynamic prices, and real results depend on your roof direction, shading and usage pattern.
