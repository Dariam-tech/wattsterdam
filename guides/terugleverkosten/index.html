Terugleverkosten: what suppliers charge for your solar power

Your supplier pays you for the solar power you feed back, then charges you for feeding it back. From 2027 the only number that matters is the difference. Here's how it works, in plain English.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Quick answer

Terugleverkosten (feed-in costs) are what your energy supplier charges for the electricity your solar panels feed back into the grid. Most fixed and variable contracts have them. The opposite is the terugleververgoeding (feed-in payment): what the supplier pays you per kWh.

They drop sharply in 2027. In 2026, with saldering, contracts charged roughly €0.11 to €0.18 per kWh fed back. Most fixed 1-year contracts on sale for 2027 charge about €0.04 to €0.07 per kWh, and pay about €0.08 to €0.09. A few pay much more but also charge much more: up to about €0.22 per kWh.

That's why the only number that matters from 2027 is the net payment: payment minus costs. For 1-year fixed contracts on sale in autumn 2026 it ranges from about €0.0025 to €0.042 per kWh. For a home feeding back 3,000 kWh, that's between €8 and €126 a year.

Why suppliers charge them

Solar panels all produce at the same time: sunny middle of the day, mostly in summer. The supplier has to take that power when market prices are lowest, and it has to balance the grid when the sun comes and goes. The regulator ACM has investigated three times. In December 2025 it concluded that the feed-in costs suppliers charge are not unreasonable compared with what they actually pay, and that it doesn't expect them to rise further while market conditions stay the same.

The three ways suppliers charge them

MethodHow it worksGood to know
Per kWhA fixed amount for every kWh you feed back.Easiest to compare. The ACM wants every supplier to use this method.
Tiers (staffel)A fixed yearly amount per band of feed-in, for example one price for 2,000 to 2,250 kWh.Many tiered suppliers charge nothing below a threshold, from a handful of kWh up to about 1,000 kWh. Good for small systems.
Higher fixed feeNo separate line; customers pay a higher monthly fee.Easy to miss when comparing. Until 2027 suppliers may spread these costs over all customers. From 2027 they may only charge them to customers with solar panels, and must show them per kWh on the invoice.

Most dynamic contracts, with hourly prices, have little or no feed-in costs, because the supplier passes the market price straight on to you. Most dynamic suppliers have said they will charge none in 2027. The ACM points out these contracts only suit people who can live with prices that change every hour.

What changes on 1 January 2027

  • Saldering ends. Today each kWh you feed back cancels a kWh you take from the grid, worth your full rate of about €0.26. From 2027 you only get the feed-in payment, and pay the full rate for everything you take.
  • A legal minimum payment. Until 1 January 2030 the feed-in payment must be at least 50% of the price you pay for electricity excluding energy tax and VAT. That minimum applies to the payment itself, before feed-in costs, so the net amount can still be very small.
  • Only solar customers pay feed-in costs. Suppliers can no longer spread them over all customers.
  • The invoice must show terugleverkosten per kWh fed back, whatever method the supplier uses. That makes contracts easier to compare.
  • The model contract (the ACM's standard contract every supplier must offer) has charged feed-in costs per kWh since 2026.

A worked example

A home feeds back 3,000 kWh in 2027. Two contracts, with numbers in line with 2027 offers:

Contract AContract B
Feed-in payment€0.17 per kWh€0.09 per kWh
Feed-in costs€0.165 per kWh€0.05 per kWh
Net per kWh€0.005€0.04
You get for 3,000 kWh€15€120

Contract A advertises almost twice the payment but pays you €105 less. Compare the net figure, and compare it together with the price you pay per kWh you take from the grid: that part of the bill is usually much bigger.

What you can do

  • Use more of your own solar power when it's produced. Run the washing machine, dishwasher and EV charging in the middle of the day. Each kWh you use directly saves you the full rate of about €0.26; each kWh you feed back earns only the net payment.
  • Find your own numbers. Your jaarafrekening shows your teruglevering in kWh. Put it into the 2027 calculator to see what the end of saldering costs you.
  • Compare on net payment per kWh, at the amount you actually feed back. A tier threshold can make a big difference for a small system. Payments are tied to a specific contract, so check the figures for the exact contract and duration you're offered.
  • Look at a dynamic contract if you can shift your use to cheap hours and accept changing prices.
