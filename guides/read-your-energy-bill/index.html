How to read your Dutch energy bill

About half of what you're charged for electricity has nothing to do with how much you use. Then a €629 tax credit quietly cancels most of it. Here is every line of the bill, in plain English, with a worked example for 2027.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Quick answer

A Dutch energy bill has two kinds of charges. Usage charges grow with every kWh you take from the grid: the supplier's price per kWh and the energy tax. Fixed charges stay the same whatever you use: the grid operator's costs and the supplier's monthly fee. Every home connection also gets a fixed tax credit of €628.69 a year in 2027, which is subtracted from the bill.

For an apartment without gas using 2,500 kWh a year, the fixed charges are about €678 of €1,320 in total charges in 2027: roughly half. After the tax credit, that home pays about €691 a year, or €58 a month.

The two bills you get

  • Termijnbedrag (or voorschot): the fixed amount you pay every month. It is an advance, based on an estimate of your yearly use.
  • Jaarafrekening (or jaarnota): the yearly statement. It compares what you actually used with what you paid in advances. You get money back or pay extra, and your monthly amount is usually reset.

Everything below appears on the jaarafrekening. The monthly advance doesn't show the breakdown.

A worked example: electricity-only apartment, 2027

2,500 kWh a year, no gas, no solar panels, in the Liander area, with a supply price of €0.15 per kWh including VAT and a supplier fee of €8 a month. Your own contract will differ; the grid costs, tax and credit are the same for everyone in the same area.

The fixed lines (€96 + €582 = €678) are 51% of all charges. That's why using less saves you less than you might expect: each kWh you save is worth about €0.26 here, while about €678 stays the same whatever you do. It's also why the tax credit matters so much: it cancels almost all of the grid costs.

Grid costs depend on where you live. The proposed 2027 amounts for electricity at the three largest grid operators are about €550 (Enexis), €557 (Stedin) and €582 (Liander) a year including VAT. The final amounts are confirmed by the regulator, ACM, at the end of the year.

If you also have gas

Gas has the same structure: a price per m³, energy tax per m³, a supplier fee and grid costs. The tax credit is given only once per home, not once for gas and once for electricity. It usually appears on the electricity part of the bill.

If you have solar panels

Until 31 December 2026 the electricity you feed back (teruglevering) is netted against what you take (levering): saldering. From 1 January 2027 that stops. You pay the full price for every kWh you take, and get only a feed-in payment for every kWh you feed back.

To see what that means for you, take the levering and teruglevering figures from your jaarafrekening and put them into the 2027 calculator.

Five things to check on your bill

  • The meter readings (meterstanden). Do they match your meter? Errors here affect everything else.
  • The tax credit line. If it's missing at your main home, contact your supplier: you are entitled to it.
  • Your supply price per kWh, compared with offers on comparison sites. It's the part you can change.
  • The new monthly advance. If it went up a lot, check whether the estimate of your use is realistic.
  • The contract end date (einddatum). After it, fixed contracts often switch to a more expensive variable rate.
